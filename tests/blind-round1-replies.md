
## Case 7
Pages opened: none

Check your bank's real deposit rate against your country's official inflation figure.

**Inflation** = prices go up over time, so the same money buys less.

Tiny example (example numbers, not real ones):
• You put 100 in the bank at 5% a year → after a year you have 105.
• Prices rose 8% → a snack that cost 1.00 now costs 1.08.
• 105 ÷ 1.08 ≈ 97.2 → your money buys about 2.8% less than before. That's your **real return** (what your money can really buy): 1.05 / 1.08 − 1 ≈ −2.8%.

So the bank isn't lying: you do get 5% more money. But if prices rise faster than 5%, each unit buys less.

Traps:
• Only compare with the official inflation number from your statistics office or central bank (verify) — not from social media.
• "High return" offers that beat inflation easily are often scams.
• Still, a deposit is safer than keeping cash at home: cash earns 0%.

*Educational only, not financial advice. Check licences on the official regulator site.*
